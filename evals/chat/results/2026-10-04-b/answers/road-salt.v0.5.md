# Why salt melts ice on roads

**Short answer:** Salt does not heat the ice. Salt dissolves in the thin layer of water on the ice and lowers the temperature at which that water freezes. The road can then stay below 0 °C, and the ice still melts.

## The mechanism

1. **Ice always has a thin film of liquid water on it.** Sun, traffic, and pressure keep a small amount of water liquid. Salt needs this water to start dissolving.
2. **At 0 °C, ice and water are in balance.** Water molecules leave the ice surface and become liquid. Other water molecules rejoin the ice. At 0 °C, the two rates are equal, so the amount of ice stays the same.
3. **Salt dissolves into ions.** NaCl splits into Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions in the water.
4. **The ions take up space at the ice surface.** Fewer water molecules touch the ice each second, so fewer rejoin it. The rate at which molecules leave the ice does not change, because salt does not enter the ice.
5. **More molecules leave than rejoin, so the ice melts.** To restore the balance, the temperature must drop further. That lower temperature is the new freezing point of the salty water (the *brine*).

This effect is called **freezing-point depression**. It depends on the number of dissolved particles, not on what they are (established fact). Sugar would also work, but each sugar molecule stays as one particle, and salt gives two.

## Worked example (ideal estimate)

Dissolve 58 g of NaCl (1 mole) in 1 kg of water.

- Particles: 1 mole NaCl → 2 moles of ions.
- Water's freezing-point constant: 1.86 °C per mole of particles per kg.
- Drop in freezing point: 2 × 1.86 ≈ **3.7 °C**.

So this brine freezes at about −3.7 °C. More salt lowers the freezing point further.

## Where it works and where it fails

| Road temperature | Result |
|---|---|
| −5 °C | Works. Brine can stay liquid well below this temperature. |
| About −10 °C | Works slowly. Road crews treat this as the practical limit for rock salt. |
| Below −21 °C | Fails. No amount of NaCl keeps the water liquid. |

The −21 °C limit is the lowest freezing point a salt–water mixture can reach (about 23% salt by mass). More salt past this point does not dissolve. Below this temperature, crews use calcium chloride (CaCl₂). CaCl₂ gives three ions per unit and stays effective to roughly −30 °C.

## One common confusion

Melting absorbs heat. The brine draws that heat from its surroundings, so salted ice gets *colder* as it melts. This is why salt and ice chill the cream in an ice-cream maker. The ice melts because its freezing point dropped, not because it warmed.
