# Why does ice float on water?

_v0.5_

**Ice floats because it is less dense than liquid water.** Water is one of very few substances whose solid form is lighter, volume for volume, than its liquid form.

## The mechanism

1. **Floating depends on density.** An object floats when it weighs less than the water it pushes aside. Density is mass per volume, so a less dense solid floats.
2. **Ice is about 9% less dense than water.** Ice is about 0.917 g/cm³. Liquid water near 0 °C is about 1.000 g/cm³.
3. **Hydrogen bonds cause the difference.** Each water molecule (H₂O) can form hydrogen bonds to four neighbors. A hydrogen bond is a weak attraction between the H of one molecule and the O of another.
4. **In ice, these bonds lock into an open lattice.** Each molecule holds its four neighbors at fixed angles. This hexagonal arrangement leaves empty space between the molecules.
5. **In liquid water, the lattice partly collapses.** Bonds break and re-form constantly. Molecules slip into the gaps, so the same mass fits into a smaller volume.

The causal chain: hydrogen bonds → open, rigid lattice in ice → more volume per molecule → lower density → ice floats.

## What the number means

The ratio 0.917 / 1.000 tells you how much of the ice sits underwater. About **92% of a floating block is submerged**, and 8% shows above the surface. This is the reason most of an iceberg is hidden. For seawater (about 1.025 g/cm³), the submerged part is about 89%.

## Why this is unusual

Most substances shrink when they freeze. Their molecules pack *closer* in the solid, so the solid sinks in its own liquid. A block of solid paraffin wax sinks in melted wax. Solid iron sinks in molten iron. Water is different because its hydrogen bonds force a spacing that is *wider* than close packing.

## A related effect

Liquid water is densest at about 4 °C, not at 0 °C. Below 4 °C, small ice-like clusters start to form and the water expands slightly. So in winter, 4 °C water sinks to the bottom of a lake, and ice forms at the top. The ice layer insulates the water below, so lakes rarely freeze solid, and fish survive.
