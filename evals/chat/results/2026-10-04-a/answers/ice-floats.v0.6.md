# Why ice floats on water

**Short answer:** Ice is about 9% less dense than liquid water. Anything less dense than the liquid around it floats. Ice is less dense because hydrogen bonds hold its molecules in an open crystal with empty space inside.

## 1. Why lower density means floating

An object in water pushes aside (displaces) some water. The displaced water pushes the object up with a force equal to the weight of that water. This is Archimedes' principle.

- If a fully submerged object weighs more than the water it displaces, it sinks.
- If it weighs less, it rises until the displaced water's weight equals its own weight. Then it floats.

Density (mass per volume) decides which case applies:

| Substance | Density near 0 °C |
|---|---|
| Liquid water | 1.000 g/cm³ |
| Ice | 0.917 g/cm³ |

**Worked example:** 1 cm³ of ice has a mass of 0.917 g. It floats when it displaces 0.917 g of water, which is 0.917 cm³. So about 92% of floating ice is below the surface. This is why most of an iceberg is hidden.

## 2. Why ice is less dense: the surprising part

Most substances become *denser* when they freeze. Their molecules slow down and pack more tightly. Solid wax, metal, and carbon dioxide all sink in their own liquid. Water is unusual, and the reason is the hydrogen bond.

**The molecule.** A water molecule (H₂O) is bent. The oxygen atom pulls electrons toward itself. This gives the oxygen a slight negative charge and each hydrogen a slight positive charge.

**The hydrogen bond.** The positive hydrogen of one molecule attracts the negative oxygen of a nearby molecule. This attraction is a hydrogen bond. Each molecule can form up to four hydrogen bonds, which point in fixed directions (a tetrahedral arrangement).

**In ice:** Every molecule holds all four hydrogen bonds in their fixed directions. The fixed angles force the molecules into a hexagonal lattice with open channels between them. The bonds hold the molecules *apart*, so the structure contains empty space.

**In liquid water:** Thermal motion breaks and re-forms hydrogen bonds continuously. Parts of the lattice collapse. Molecules move into the gaps that the rigid lattice kept empty. So the same number of molecules takes less volume.

**Numbers:** 1000 g of liquid water takes about 1000 cm³. Frozen, the same 1000 g takes about 1090 cm³. That 9% expansion is also the reason frozen pipes burst.

## 3. Where the rule fails

The claim "ice floats" depends on the open hexagonal lattice. Remove that condition, and the result changes. At very high pressures (thousands of atmospheres), water freezes into other crystal forms, such as ice VI and ice VII. Pressure forces their molecules into compact arrangements. These forms are denser than liquid water and sink. They do not form on Earth's surface, but they probably exist deep inside some icy moons.

## 4. Why it matters

Liquid water is densest at about 4 °C. In winter, lake water at 4 °C sinks to the bottom. Ice forms on top and floats there. The ice layer insulates the water below, so lakes rarely freeze solid, and aquatic life survives the winter.
