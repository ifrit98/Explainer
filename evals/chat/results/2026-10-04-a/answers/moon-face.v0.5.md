# Why we always see the same side of the Moon

**Short answer:** The Moon turns once on its axis in exactly the time it takes to orbit Earth, about 27.3 days. The same face therefore always points at us. Earth's gravity caused this match. The name for it is **tidal locking**.

## The common confusion: the Moon does rotate

Many people think the Moon does not spin. That is incorrect.

- **If the Moon did not rotate**, it would keep facing one fixed direction in space as it went around Earth. Over one month, we would see every side of it.
- **Because it rotates once per orbit**, it turns by the same angle that it moves around Earth. Its face stays pointed at us.

Test this yourself. Walk in a circle around a chair and keep your face toward the chair. When you finish, you have also turned once completely around.

## Why the rotation and the orbit match

This is the causal chain. Observations and physics support it as established fact.

1. **Earth stretches the Moon.** Gravity gets weaker with distance. Earth pulls harder on the Moon's near side than on its far side. This stretches the Moon slightly along the Earth–Moon line and makes a **tidal bulge**.
2. **A spinning Moon carries its bulge out of line.** Suppose the young Moon rotated faster than it orbited. Its rotation would drag the bulge ahead of the Earth–Moon line. Rock takes time to deform, so the bulge cannot realign immediately.
3. **Earth pulls the bulge back.** Earth's gravity pulls on the misaligned bulge. This pull makes a **torque**, a twisting force, that opposes the Moon's spin.
4. **The spin slows until the bulge stays aligned.** The torque stops only when the bulge no longer moves relative to Earth. That happens when one rotation takes exactly one orbit.
5. **The locked state is stable.** If the rotation drifts a little, the bulge moves out of line again. The torque then pushes the rotation back to the match.

The Moon probably became locked early in its history. Published estimates are uncertain, but they are short compared to the Moon's age of about 4.5 billion years.

## The same effect works in the other direction

The Moon also raises tides on Earth, and these tides slow Earth's rotation.

- Earth's day becomes longer by about 2 milliseconds per century. This is an established measurement.
- The Moon moves away from Earth by about 3.8 cm per year. Lunar laser ranging measures this.

Earth is not locked yet, because Earth is much larger and the Moon's pull on it is weaker. Pluto and its moon Charon show the end state: each always shows the same face to the other.

## A small correction: we see about 59%, not 50%

The Moon's orbit is slightly elliptical, and its axis is slightly tilted. The Moon therefore appears to rock a little over each month. This rocking is called **libration**. Because of libration, we see about 59% of the Moon's surface over time. The remaining 41% is never visible from Earth.
