# Why does ice float on water?

_none_

Ice floats because it is **less dense than liquid water**, about 0.92 g/cm³ compared to roughly 1.00 g/cm³. That's unusual: most substances are denser as solids than as liquids.

### The reason: hydrogen bonding

1. **Water molecules are polar.** The oxygen atom carries a slight negative charge, and the two hydrogen atoms carry slight positive charges.

2. **Hydrogen bonds form between molecules.** The hydrogen of one molecule is attracted to the oxygen of a neighboring molecule.

3. **In liquid water**, these bonds constantly break and re-form. The molecules tumble around and can pack fairly closely together.

4. **When water freezes**, each molecule locks into a fixed position, hydrogen-bonded to four neighbors in a tetrahedral arrangement. This forms a **hexagonal crystal lattice** with a lot of open space inside it.

5. **That open structure takes up about 9% more volume** than the same mass of liquid water. Same mass, more volume means lower density.

### Why lower density means floating

By **Archimedes' principle**, a floating object sinks until it displaces a weight of water equal to its own weight. Since ice is about 92% as dense as water, roughly 90% of a floating chunk of ice sits below the surface. That's where the phrase "tip of the iceberg" comes from.

### Why it matters

- **Lakes freeze from the top down.** The floating ice insulates the water below, so aquatic life can survive the winter.
- Water is densest at about **4 °C**, so in cold lakes the bottom water often stays around that temperature.
- The expansion on freezing is also why **pipes burst** and why freeze-thaw cycles crack rocks and roads.
