# Why does ice float on water?

_v0.5_

Ice floats because it is **less dense** than liquid water. Ice is less dense because hydrogen bonds lock water molecules into an open crystal that holds them farther apart than they sit in the liquid.

## The causal chain

1. **Buoyancy decides floating.** An object in water feels an upward force equal to the weight of the water it pushes aside. This is Archimedes' principle, an established fact. An object floats when it is less dense than the liquid, because it can push aside its own weight of liquid before it is fully submerged.

2. **Ice is less dense than water.** These are measured values:
   - Liquid water at 0 °C: about **1.000 g/cm³**
   - Ice at 0 °C: about **0.917 g/cm³**

   So water expands by about 9% when it freezes. The same mass takes more space.

3. **Hydrogen bonds cause the expansion.** A water molecule (H₂O) is bent. Its oxygen end is slightly negative and its hydrogen ends are slightly positive. A *hydrogen bond* is the attraction between a hydrogen on one molecule and an oxygen on a neighbor.
   - **In ice**, each molecule forms 4 hydrogen bonds at fixed angles. The molecules lock into a hexagonal lattice with large open gaps.
   - **In liquid water**, the bonds constantly break and re-form. The lattice collapses partly, and molecules slip into the gaps. More molecules fit in the same volume, so the liquid is denser.

## What the number tells you

The ratio 0.917 / 1.000 is the fraction of a floating ice block that sits below the surface. About **92% of an iceberg is underwater**, and only about 8% shows. Seawater is denser (about 1.025 g/cm³), so in the sea the submerged fraction drops to about 89%.

## Why this is unusual

Most substances do the opposite. Their solid is *denser* than their liquid, because cooling packs the molecules tighter. For example, a block of solid paraffin wax sinks in melted wax. Water breaks this rule only because its hydrogen bonds force the open lattice.

Two cases test the explanation:
- **Holds:** In normal ice, the open lattice gives 0.917 g/cm³, so ice floats.
- **Breaks when you change the mass, not the structure:** Heavy-water ice (D₂O) has the same open lattice, but deuterium is heavier than hydrogen. Its density is about 1.02 g/cm³, so heavy-water ice **sinks** in ordinary water. Floating depends on density, not on the word "ice."

## One consequence

Liquid water is densest at about **4 °C**. In winter, a lake's 4 °C water sinks to the bottom, and ice forms on top. The ice then insulates the water below, so lakes freeze from the top down and fish survive the winter.

**Not covered here:** high-pressure ice forms (for example, ice VII inside some planets) use denser packings and do sink. Look up "phases of ice" for details.
