How greenhouse gases warm the Earth: interactive models

This page goes with explanation.md. Each model below is a few lines of arithmetic. Move the sliders and the numbers and diagrams update. All fluxes are global averages in W/m².

Model 1: one absorbing layer

The atmosphere is modelled as one layer. Sunlight passes straight through it. It absorbs a fraction ε of the infrared coming up from the surface, and it emits ε·σTa⁴ both up and down. Start at ε = 0, then increase it, and watch the surface temperature and the downward infrared.

Things to notice: (1) at ε = 0 the surface is 255 K, the temperature set by energy balance alone. (2) Around ε ≈ 0.78 it reaches the observed 288 K. (3) The model cannot go above ε = 1. The real atmosphere is already nearly opaque at the centre of the CO₂ band and still warms when CO₂ is added, which is why Model 2 is needed.

Model 2: emission height and lapse rate

Temperature falls by about Γ = 6.5 K/km with height. On average the Earth radiates to space from the level where the air is 255 K, about 5 km up. Adding CO₂ lifts that level into colder air, so less energy leaves the planet (the forcing, ΔF = 5.35·ln(C/280)). The planet warms until the outgoing radiation balances the incoming again.

Gray line: pre-industrial temperature profile. Red line: the profile after the planet has warmed by ΔT. Dots: the effective emission height. With no feedbacks (λ = 3.2), the emission level has risen by ΔT/Γ but is back at 255 K, so outgoing radiation balances again. A doubling raises it by under 200 m. The effect is small on this scale, but it accounts for all of the warming.
