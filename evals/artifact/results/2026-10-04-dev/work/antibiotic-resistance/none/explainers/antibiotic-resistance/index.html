Why concentration matters: net growth rate vs. drug level

This chart shows how fast each type grows (above zero) or dies (below zero) at each drug concentration, ignoring crowding. The shaded band is the mutant selection window: concentrations high enough to kill susceptible cells but too low to stop resistant ones. The dashed line marks the concentration you've chosen above.

What to try

  • No drug: resistant mutants appear but stay a tiny minority. With a fitness cost, they're outcompeted.
  • In the window: the susceptible population collapses and the resistant line, which started from a handful of cells, climbs to take over. Press "Run again" a few times. When the first mutants appear, and whether they survive, changes from run to run.
  • Above both MICs: both types die. (Real drugs can't always reach this level safely in every part of the body. Bacteria can also climb out of this regime one mutation step at a time.)
  • Short treatment in the window: the drug stops before the susceptible cells are gone, and both types regrow. The resistant fraction is now far higher than before treatment.
  • Lower the mutation rate to 10⁻¹⁰: in many runs, no resistant cell exists when the drug arrives, and treatment succeeds. Resistance is a numbers game: (population size) × (mutation rate).
How the model works

Time advances in steps of 0.02 hours. Each type i has a birth rate g_i × (1 − N/K), where N is the total population and K = 10¹⁰ is the carrying capacity. It also has a drug-caused death rate

kill_i(c) = (g_max − g_min) · x^κ / (x^κ − g_min/g_max),   with x = c / MIC_i

This is a standard pharmacodynamic curve, chosen so that net growth is exactly zero at the MIC. The parameters are g_max = 1/hour for S (about one doubling every 42 minutes), g_min = −3/hour, κ = 2. The resistant type's g is reduced by the fitness cost, and its MIC is 64× higher.

Births and deaths are drawn at random (Poisson) while a type has fewer than 10,000 cells, so small resistant lineages can die out by chance. Above that size the model switches to the deterministic average. In each step, every susceptible birth has probability μ of producing a resistant cell instead. There is no immune system and no horizontal gene transfer, so this is deliberately the simplest case.
